Privacy policy

In force from 24 November 2026 · iOS and Android apps

Version française

In short

  • No sign-up
  • No ads
  • No tracking
  • Nothing sold

Tult works without signing up. Your players, games and statistics are kept on your phone. A copy of your games is also kept online, so your friends find them in their statistics and you find yours on another phone. Only your profile photo is shown to your friends: your game photos stay on your phone. A live tournament can be seen by anyone who has its code. The participants an organiser records in an organiser space stay there: they never become the organiser’s friends and don’t enter anyone’s statistics.

Who publishes the app

Tult is published by Maxime Lemaitre, in France, who is responsible for the processing described here. For any question about this policy: jointultapp@gmail.com.

Service providers

For this online data, Tult uses service providers who act on its behalf and on its instructions:

  • Supabase (Supabase Inc.) hosts the database and manages the accounts.
  • Resend (Resend Inc.) sends the email containing your sign-in code, if you link your account with an email address. It receives your address and that code.
  • Google identifies you if you choose to sign in with Google, under its own privacy policy. Tult then receives your email address.
  • Cloudflare (Cloudflare Inc.) hosts the tult.app website, including the live tournament pages. Like any host, it receives the IP address of the visitors of these pages.
  • Sentry (Functional Software, Inc.) receives the crash reports, which it stores in the European Union (see “Crash reports”).

For voice entry, the phrase said may also be turned into text by the speech recognition of Google (on Android) or Apple (on iPhone), under their own terms and privacy policy (see “Voice: announcements and voice entry”).

For the premium subscription, our server also asks Google Play about your purchases, with the purchase token Google gave the app (see “Premium subscription”).

These companies are based in the United States. Transfers of data outside the European Union are covered by the European Commission’s standard contractual clauses. No data is sold or used for advertising.

Legal basis

This processing is needed to provide the service you use: count your games, share them with the players concerned, let you find them again and provide the premium option if you buy it (Article 6(1)(b) of the GDPR).

The names of the participants an organiser records in an organiser space are processed for the organiser’s legitimate interest in keeping the scores and standings of their contest (Article 6(1)(f) of the GDPR). Each participant can object and ask for their deletion (see « Organiser spaces »).

Sending the phrase said to Google or Apple for voice entry relies on your consent (Article 6(1)(a) of the GDPR), asked once on each device, which you can withdraw at any time in the settings.

Crash reports rely on Tult’s legitimate interest in fixing its app’s errors (Article 6(1)(f) of the GDPR). You can object by turning them off in the settings.

Permissions requested

  • Camera: to scan a QR code and, if you wish, take an avatar photo or photos during and at the end of a game.
  • Photos: to pick an avatar image, a souvenir photo or an organiser space’s logo from your gallery.
  • Microphone: for voice entry of the scores, only when you tap the microphone, for the length of one phrase.
  • Speech recognition (iPhone): to turn the phrase said for voice entry into text.

These permissions are only requested when you use the feature concerned. You can refuse or withdraw them at any time in your phone’s settings; the rest of the app keeps working. The images you pick are copied into the app; they only leave it in a backup you create yourself, except your profile photo, shown to your friends in a reduced version.

What you choose to share

  • QR code, friend code and link: your QR code contains your name, colour, emoji, friend code and account identifier, never your photo. A guest’s contains their name, colour and emoji. The link that lets a guest find their games works only once and expires after 7 days.
  • Results card: when you share an image at the end of a game or tournament, it shows the names, scores and ratings, but not the players’ photos. It goes through your phone’s share menu. The app or service you choose (messaging, social network…) then applies its own privacy policy.
  • Game photos: the photos taken during or at the end of a game stay on your phone. They only leave it if you share the one chosen as the souvenir (with a banner showing the game, the winner and the date), through your phone’s share menu, or in a backup.
  • Backing up your data: from the settings, the app can create a file containing your players and your games (photos included), your tournaments, your challenges, your organiser spaces (logos and participants included) and your settings. You choose where to save or send it (Files, iCloud Drive, Google Drive, email…); that service then applies its own privacy policy. The file is not encrypted: keep it somewhere safe. It is only used to restore your data in Tult, on this phone or another one.
  • Review request: after a few games, the app may show the App Store or Google Play rating window. If you leave a rating or a review, it is sent to Apple or Google and published under their terms; the app receives nothing from it.

Live tournaments

With the premium option, a tournament’s organiser can show it on a big screen and let the participants follow it, on tult.app, with the tournament’s code or its QR code. These pages show the tournament’s name, its place if entered, the team and player names, their colours and emojis, the matches, the courts and the scores. They also show a small version (96 pixels) of the photo of each player who has one: it is sent with the live tournament, shown beside their name, and erased with it. Anyone who has the code can see them, without an account: only put in names and photos the participants agree to see on display (remove a player’s photo before the tournament if they don’t want it shown).

On the participants’ page, the team you follow is remembered on your phone, or in your browser on the web. In the app, if you say which of the players you are, the tournament counts in your statistics once it is over.

During the tournament, these pages also show its statistics, worked out on the organiser’s phone from the results and the games played in the app: for instance a player’s best average, the number of 180s, the best checkout, a comeback or the closest match, with the player’s or team’s name. If the organiser puts a match « on the big screen » (featured), its score is published live, visit by visit or point by point (points left, last visit, average, legs, possible checkout). Like the rest of the page, this can be seen by anyone who has the tournament’s code.

The organiser can feature up to two matches and give each to a « scorer »: another phone, without the premium option, which joins with a 6-digit code valid for 12 hours, in the app or simply in its browser, on tult.app. In the browser, the match is kept in that browser’s storage, on that phone only, while it is scored. That phone’s (or browser’s) invisible account is then recorded as the scorer of that match, and only it, with the organiser, can change it. The throws the scorer enters go through our server to the organiser’s phone, which records the game in its tournament; they are shown to nobody else. The match goes neither in the scorer’s history nor in their statistics. The link between the scorer and the match is erased with the live tournament.

For a contest of an organiser space, these pages also show the space’s name, colour and logo. Once a tournament is over, the organiser can also show a results code: participants who have the app scan it and say which of the players they are, so the tournament counts in their statistics. The organiser doesn’t see who did.

A live tournament is no longer shown 30 days after its last change. It is erased, with its featured match and its throws, when the organiser deletes it or deletes their account.

Organiser spaces

With the premium option, you can create organiser spaces, for a belote contest or a club for instance, next to your Personal space. Each space has its own participants, games, tournaments and nights. Its participants never become your friends, don’t show in your friends list and don’t count in your personal statistics; your own games in the space only count there if you chose so. A participant can’t be linked to an account or get a QR code.

If you record the participants of a contest, a club or an association, you choose the names entered: let them know their names and results are recorded in Tult and, for a live tournament, shown to anyone who has its code. A first name or a nickname is enough; for minors, prefer a first name and an initial.

From a participant’s page, « Delete with their data » erases their name, emoji, photo and the games they played alone, on your phone and online; their places in other people’s games stay, without a name, so the standings still hold. A participant can ask the organiser, or write to jointultapp@gmail.com: we erase what is kept online and let the organiser know.

If the premium option stops, your organiser spaces can still be viewed but no longer changed, and nothing is deleted. They are erased online, with their participants, games and tournaments, when you delete your account.

Premium subscription

The premium option is a one-year subscription, renewed every year, which you can buy in the app on Android, through Google Play. The payment is handled entirely by Google, under its terms and privacy policy: the app sees neither your name, nor your Google account, nor your payment details. It only gives Google Play the technical identifier of your Tult account, to tie the subscription to that account.

After the purchase, the app sends our server the purchase token Google Play gives it. The server asks Google whether the subscription is real and paid, and until when, then confirms the purchase to Google. It keeps what Google says about it: the purchase token, the order number, the subscription’s state (active, cancelled, on hold, expired), its end date, whether it renews and whether it is a test purchase. At most once a day, when you open the app, it checks again with Google, to follow a renewal, a cancellation or a refund.

« Restore my purchases », on the premium page, checks the subscriptions of this phone’s Google Play account again and ties the subscription to this phone’s Tult account: a subscription serves one Tult account at a time. If you link your account, the subscription follows you.

You manage and cancel the subscription in Google Play, or from the premium page (« Manage my subscription »): premium lasts until the end of the year paid for. A refund is asked from Google. This information is kept as long as your account exists, then erased with it. Deleting your Tult account does not stop the subscription with Google: cancel it in Google Play first.

Tips

From the settings, you can leave a tip. The payment is handled entirely by Apple (App Store) or Google (Google Play), under their terms and privacy policy: the app sees neither your name, nor your account, nor your payment details. It only remembers, on your phone, the date of your last tip, to thank you. A tip unlocks no feature and is not a subscription.

Voice: announcements and voice entry

If you turn on voice announcements, the scores are read by the text-to-speech engine built into your phone (iOS or Android), with nothing sent.

With voice entry, you say the points instead of tapping them (“point Max”, “five three”, “one forty”). The app only listens when you tap the microphone, for the length of one phrase, never in the background. Tult neither records nor keeps the sound: the app only reads the recognised text, then records the point it gives, as if it had been tapped. The names of the game’s players are given to the speech recognition, so that it recognises them better.

The phrase is turned into text by your phone’s speech recognition. When it can do so without the internet, nothing leaves the device. Otherwise, the phrase is sent to Google (on Android) or Apple (on iPhone), only if you agreed on this device and it is connected to the internet. It is then covered by Google’s or Apple’s privacy policy. You can withdraw this agreement at any time in the settings (Voice › Voice entry: Google online), or refuse the microphone in your phone’s settings: the rest of the app works without the voice.

The Slate

If you write your points by hand on the Slate, your writing is read on your phone by ML Kit, a Google tool built into the app. The handwriting model (about 20 MB) is downloaded once from Google’s servers, when you ask for it. Your strokes never leave the phone and are not kept: only the number you confirm is saved, as if it had been typed.

As with all its ML Kit tools, Google receives technical measurements, for diagnostics and usage statistics: phone model and version, app name and version, an installation identifier not used to identify you, the model’s language, how long readings take, and errors. They are encrypted in transit, Google passes them on to no one, and they fall under Google’s privacy policy. If you would rather send nothing, do not turn on the Slate: the keyboard works without it.

Crash reports

If Tult or one of its screens crashes, a technical report is sent to Sentry (Functional Software, Inc.), which stores it in the European Union (Frankfurt). It contains the error and the place in the code, the app’s version and build number, the phone’s model and system, the screens open just before (without their content) and, for a system crash, a random identifier created at installation, not linked to your account.

It never contains your players’ names, your scores, your games, your photos or your account’s identifier. Your IP address is not kept, and no screenshot or recording of your use is made. Nothing is sent unless the app crashes. Without a connection, the report waits on the phone and goes later.

These reports are only used to fix bugs and are kept for 90 days at most. You can turn them off in the settings (About › Crash reports).

Other players

If you add people close to you as players, especially with their photo, make sure they agree. Their names, colours and emojis are kept online with your games, and are only visible to the people who played those games, except in a live tournament, which anyone with its code can see, with their matches’ statistics and, for the featured match, their live score: let them know. Their photos stay on your phone, except in a live tournament you run, where they are shown small beside their names: ask them first. For the participants of a contest, see « Organiser spaces ».

During voice entry, the microphone may also hear the people around the table, for the length of the phrase. Nothing of it is kept, but where the phrase goes to Google or Apple, what they say at that moment goes with it.

How long data is kept, and deleting it

On your phone, data stays until you delete it. You can delete a game or a tournament from its screen, or by swiping it to the left in the lists. A removed player no longer shows in the lists but stays linked to their past games. To erase everything from the phone, use « Erase all data » in the settings, or uninstall the app.

Online, data is kept as long as your account exists. A game you delete is also deleted for the other players; a friend’s game you remove from your statistics only goes for you, and the other players of that game see your name in grey. To delete your account, open the settings and choose « Delete my account »: your profile, profile photo, friend code, figures per game, friends, players, games, organiser spaces (with their participants) and the record of your premium subscription online are erased, and the games played with people who have an account stay in their statistics, with no link to you. Deleting your account does not stop a premium subscription: cancel it in Google Play. Uninstalling the app does not delete this online data. You can also ask for deletion from this page or by writing to jointultapp@gmail.com.

Your phone’s backups (iCloud or Google) may include the app’s data, depending on your system settings. They then fall under Apple’s or Google’s policy.

Children

The app can be used by the whole family. It asks for no age or real name, and uses no data for advertising. Before linking an account to an email address, a child under 15 needs a parent’s agreement. An organiser who records participants who are minors keeps to a first name and an initial.

Your rights

The General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR) gives you a right of access, rectification, erasure, portability and objection. You can view, change, export and delete your information directly in the app, and delete your account in the settings. For any other request, write to jointultapp@gmail.com: you will get an answer within one month. You can also lodge a complaint with the CNIL, the French data protection authority.

Changes

This policy is updated when the app changes. The current version is always published on this page, with the date it comes into force.
